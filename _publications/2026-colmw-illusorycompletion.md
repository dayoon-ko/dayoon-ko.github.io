We study multi-constraint problems where valid answers must satisfy several constraints simultaneously, and find that illusory completion frequently occurs, wherein agents believe tasks are complete despite unresolved or violated constraints, leading to underverified answers. We introduce the Epistemic Ledger, a trajectory-level method that tracks evidence and agent belief for each candidate–constraint pair, revealing four recurring failure patterns: bare assertions, overlooked refutations, stagnation, and premature exit. We further propose LiveLedger, an inference-time constraint tracker that reduces underverified answers by up to 26.5 points and improves accuracy by up to 11.6 points.
